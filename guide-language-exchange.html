LANGUAGE EXCHANGE

How to turn a language exchange into a real friendship

Balance practice, curiosity and boundaries so the connection can grow beyond small talk.

Written and reviewed by Nadiia Honda · Published 1 September 2026 · 11 minute read

Language exchange gives two strangers a reason to meet, but friendship needs more than correcting grammar. The shift happens when both people feel seen as people — not only as free teachers.

Set a fair structure

Agree on a simple split, such as thirty minutes in each language. Ask how much correction the other person wants. Some people prefer notes after they finish speaking; constant interruption can make conversation tense.

A clear structure protects both sides from feeling used or exhausted.

Bring one real topic

Choose something connected to daily life: a neighbourhood tradition, a recent film, food from home or a small goal for the month. Real topics reveal personality and prevent every meeting from becoming the same introduction.

Prepare one question or story in advance so you are not starting from zero each time.

Notice mutual effort

Healthy exchange feels balanced. Both people ask questions, respect time and help each other. If one person only receives tutoring or repeatedly crosses boundaries, it is reasonable to end the arrangement.

Keep the next step small, specific and easy for both people to decline.

Add a shared activity

After two or three comfortable sessions, try cooking, a museum, a walk or a public event. The language is still present, but the relationship gains shared memories. Shared activity often moves the connection from “practice partners” to “people who enjoy each other’s company.”
