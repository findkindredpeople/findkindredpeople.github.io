LANGUAGE EXCHANGE

How to turn a language exchange into a real friendship

Choose a format that matches what you want to practise. Agree time, corrections and boundaries before expecting a language exchange to become friendship.

Published by Nadiia Honda · Prepared with AI assistance · Updated 11 October 2026 · 5 minutes read

A class, a hosted language café and a two-person exchange solve different problems. A group may offer conversation without an equal split between two languages; a tandem needs an agreement between two learners. First decide whether you need teaching, informal practice or a reciprocal exchange. Friendship is a possible development, rather than a service someone owes you.

Choose a format before finding a partner

Language-learning formats have different agreements
FormatWhat to checkWhat not to assume
Adult-education courseLevel, teaching language, full price, dates and places.A course may be a fixed cohort; late entry and a trial are not automatic.
Hosted language caféPractice language, next session, facilitator and newcomer rules.It may focus on one language rather than equal time for everyone’s native language.
Two-person tandemEach person’s goal, time split, correction style and public venue.Neither person has agreed to be a teacher, translator or a friend outside practice.

Real example: three SprachRaum windows

The Berlin library organiser describes SprachRaum as informal German practice with no required prior level. Its published windows below are free and do not require registration. A weekly schedule is not an individually confirmed future appointment; ask the library about your intended date and any access need.

Read the organiser’s SprachRaum page. If you have only an hour including travel, none of the full windows fits. Ask whether a shorter visit is acceptable, choose another date or investigate another format. The Berlin activity finder preserves unconfirmed dates and other missing details.

An English-language book discussion is a different activity: it can offer discussion in English, but does not promise German practice or reciprocal language time. Choose by the stated purpose, not by the general label “international”.

Agree a two-person exchange explicitly

Fictional example: Mira wants to practise German and Robin wants to practise English. Both have one hour at an agreed public venue. They choose an everyday topic and ask whether corrections should happen immediately or after a thought is finished.

One possible structure for a 60-minute tandem
MinutesPurposeAgreement
0–5Choose a topic and correction style.Each person states what they want to practise.
5–30First language.Let the learner finish; use the agreed corrections.
30–55Second language.Switch at the agreed time, even if the first conversation was enjoyable.
55–60Review.Ask whether another session is wanted; no commitment is automatic.

The split can be shorter or unequal if both people agree. Different language levels do not justify taking all the practice time. Keep dates, venue, travel and any cost outside the language-time calculation when planning your whole visit.

English proposal

“Would you like to practise at [public place] on [day/time] for an hour? We could use 25 minutes for each language and 10 minutes to choose a topic and review. Would you prefer corrections during speaking or afterwards?”

Deutscher Vorschlag

„Möchtest du am [Tag] um [Uhrzeit] eine Stunde an [öffentlicher Ort] üben? Wir könnten jeweils 25 Minuten pro Sprache und zehn Minuten für Themenwahl und Rückblick einplanen. Möchtest du direkt oder erst danach korrigiert werden?“

Replace the brackets and adjust the arrangement before sending. Kindred does not send this message or contact a partner.

When the exchange stops meeting your needs

If the first language takes all the time, refer to the agreement: “We planned to switch now. Shall we use the rest for [language]?” If correction interrupts every sentence, ask to finish a thought first. If a topic feels private, choose another one. Repeated difficulty is a reason to renegotiate or stop the arrangement.

You can decline translating personal documents, giving professional advice or preparing lessons outside the session. Agree a manageable contribution rather than accepting work you cannot sustain.

Offer friendship without attaching a condition

After mutually welcome conversation, you might suggest a separate public activity connected to a shared interest: “You mentioned that exhibition. Would you like to look at the dates and price together?” The other person can prefer language practice only. Keep the exchange if it still suits both of you, or end it respectfully if your goals differ.

Your next step is to choose one format and clarify its largest unknown. For a group, use the organiser question draft. For a tandem, agree the time split and correction style directly with the other person.
