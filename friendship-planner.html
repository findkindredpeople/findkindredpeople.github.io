YOUR WORKING PLAN

Find one place worth returning to

Sample: 60 minutes across the week, in person, with a quiet setting. Add travel time separately.

Choose a public place and check the organiser, cost, access and next date before going. You can leave early or change the plan.

  1. Day 1 · 5 min

    Choose one interest, one realistic time slot and your travel limit.

  2. Day 2 · 10 min

    Find two recurring options, such as a library discussion or a neighbourhood group. Check their next dates.

  3. Day 3 · Rest

    Leave today free. You do not need a new social task every day.

  4. Day 4 · 30 min

    Try a short, drop-in visit if available. Otherwise ask the organiser about the next session. A longer event can wait for another week.

  5. Day 5 · 5 min

    Write down what felt welcoming and whether returning would fit your routine.

  6. Day 6 · Rest

    Keep the day open. An unanswered message does not require another message.

  7. Day 7 · 10 min

    Choose one next date, or replace an unsuitable option. Measure a useful next step, not the number of new friends.

Use the plan as a decision, not a test

The tool combines your starting point, time budget and preferred setting with Kindred’s suggested steps. It does not search live events, find members, diagnose social needs or measure compatibility. Two people with the same choices receive the same starting plan; edit it for your circumstances.

What counts as a useful result?

If you are new somewhere, a confirmed next session can be enough. If you have met someone, a clear invitation gives them something they can answer. If you already have a friend, agreeing on a manageable rhythm may be more useful than another long conversation about being busy.

Make a smaller week work

With 30 minutes, choose a brief call, a message or checking one group. Do not try to squeeze a long class into a short slot. For an in-person plan, include the journey, accessibility needs, preparation and recovery in your own calendar. If those costs are too high, shorten the contact or arrange a future date.

If the other person is unavailable

A draft is an invitation, not an appointment. Only meet or call after both people agree. Accept a decline without trying to persuade them. After silence, one later follow-up can be reasonable; if there is still no reply, leave the next step with them. The follow-up guide shows how to respond to different answers.

Check a place before you commit

Already have two options? Compare their time, full visit cost and joining requirements before choosing a session.

Look for the next date, an identifiable organiser, a clear cost, an accessible venue and an explanation of how newcomers join. Do not pay simply because a listing promises instant friendship. Compare options using the recurring-group worksheet.

Keep your invitation private

Use an agreed contact channel. Avoid adding someone to a group chat without asking, publishing their number or including your exact home address. For first meetings, choose a public place and keep your own way home. For online contact, check the room and recording rules before joining.

What happens to my checklist?

Ticked steps last only while this page is open. Copy, download or print your plan if you want to keep it. Downloaded text and printed pages include the current plan and invitation; store them somewhere appropriate. The planner has no account requirement and does not automatically send messages or book activities.
