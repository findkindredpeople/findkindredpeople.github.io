SAFETY GUIDE

How to meet new people with confidence

A practical safety checklist for meeting new people from online communities.

By Nadiia Honda · Updated 31 August 2026 · 7 minute read

Most first meetings are ordinary and positive, but a few simple choices make them easier to leave, easier to verify and less stressful.

Choose the setting

Meet during open hours in a busy café, library, market, museum or organized activity. Avoid a private home, isolated park or long car journey for a first meeting. Choose a place you can reach and leave independently.

Keep control of transport

Know your route home and keep enough battery and money for transport. Do not rely on a new acquaintance for a ride. Share the time and location with someone you trust.

Protect personal information

A city and first name may be enough at the beginning. Do not share a home address, workplace schedule, identity documents, financial information or account passwords. Be cautious when someone creates urgency around money or personal access.

Plan a short first meeting

A forty-five-minute coffee or group walk is easier than an open-ended evening. If everyone feels comfortable, another plan can follow. A short plan also creates a natural exit.

Notice boundary testing

Repeated pressure, sexual comments, requests for money, attempts to isolate you, anger at a reasonable “no,” or major inconsistencies are reasons to leave. You do not need proof of danger to end a meeting.

Trust discomfort. Politeness is never more important than your safety.

After the meeting

Block or report a person when needed. Save relevant messages if you may need to document harassment or fraud. In immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

For group organizers

Use clear meeting details, public venues, visible hosts and community standards. Provide a reporting route and respond promptly. Do not publish attendee contact details without consent.
