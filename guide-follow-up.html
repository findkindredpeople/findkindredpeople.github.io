FOLLOWING UP

What to text after meeting someone new

Simple follow-up messages that feel warm, specific and never pushy.

Published by Kindred · Prepared with AI assistance · Updated 19 September 2026 · 3 minutes read

A useful follow-up makes the next step easy to understand. Remind the person how you met, mention one real detail and offer a small plan. You are checking mutual interest, not trying to write a message that guarantees a yes.

Use context, a detail and an invitation

Use the contact channel you agreed to exchange. Meeting at a group does not automatically give permission to search for private contact details or message someone through several accounts.

A first follow-up

“Hi [name], it’s [your name] from Saturday’s book group. I enjoyed our conversation about [book]. Are you going next month? If you are, would you like a 20-minute coffee beforehand at [public café]?”

Send the message while you can still refer naturally to the conversation. There is no universal deadline or best hour. A clear message a little later is more useful than a rushed message sent to satisfy a rule. If some time has passed, briefly identify yourself again.

Respond to what they actually say

Possible replies and a proportionate next step
Their responseYour next stepExample
A clear yesConfirm time, public place and expected duration.“Great—Saturday at 11 outside [venue], for about half an hour?”
Busy, with another dateAccept it if it suits you, or suggest one alternative.“Tuesday works. Shall we meet after class?”
Busy, without an alternativeLeave room for them to return with a plan.“No problem. Let me know if another day works.”
A polite but vague answerClarify once if needed; do not treat enthusiasm as a booking.“Would Thursday after the session suit you?”
A declineAccept it without bargaining or demanding a reason.“Thanks for letting me know. See you at the group.”
No replyWait; consider one later follow-up, then stop.“Just checking whether this reached you. No worries if this week doesn’t work.”

Adapt the invitation to the situation

After a group activity

“I enjoyed talking with you after the walk. I’m planning to join the same group next Sunday. Would you like to meet at the public starting point a few minutes early?”

After an online conversation

“It was good discussing [topic]. Would you like a 20-minute call on [day] at [time and time zone]? Text is also fine if you prefer.” Check that the proposed platform works for both people before sending a room link.

When your week is full

“I’d like to continue our conversation, but this week is packed. Would a short catch-up after class next Thursday work?” Being clear about limited time can make the invitation easier to accept.

A simple German version

“Hallo [Name], ich fand unser Gespräch über [Thema] bei [Veranstaltung] schön. Hast du am [Tag] nach dem Kurs Zeit für einen kurzen Kaffee bei [öffentlicher Ort]? Wenn es nicht passt, ist das auch völlig in Ordnung.”

These are templates, not messages from real members. Replace the bracketed details and keep only wording you would naturally use. The friendship planner provides an English or German draft alongside a weekly plan.

Do not turn silence into a campaign

People miss messages, lose interest or have demands you cannot see. None of those possibilities can be diagnosed from a read receipt. Give them time; one later reminder can be reasonable if the contact was welcome and they have not declined. If it remains unanswered, leave the next step with them.

A time-sensitive plan needs a cutoff: “I’ll confirm with the venue on Friday, so I’ll assume this time doesn’t work if I haven’t heard.” Do not arrive expecting someone who never agreed. Continue with your own activity or another confirmed plan.

If plans are cancelled

When you cancel, say so as soon as possible and offer a realistic alternative if you still want to meet. When they cancel, notice whether they help find another time. One cancellation is different from a pattern in which you organise everything.

After repeated cancellations without alternatives, reduce the effort: “Let me know when you have a day that works.” That is a clear handover, not a punishment.

A final check before sending

  • Will they know who I am?
  • Does the message refer to something we actually discussed?
  • Is there one clear invitation, with no hidden expense or pressure?
  • Can they say no without having to defend themselves?
  • Am I using a contact channel they agreed to share?
