BALANCE

When friendship feels one-sided

How to notice imbalance, protect your energy and decide whether to stay or step back — without drama or self-blame.

Published by Nadiia Honda · Published 1 September 2026 · Updated 3 October 2026 · Reading time

Describe what happened before explaining why

“I suggested the last three plans” describes an observation. “They do not care about me” interprets it. You may need to adjust your effort even when you do not know the reason for a pattern.

Consider the wider contact: who proposes alternatives, remembers an ordinary detail, listens and arrives when a meeting is agreed? Effort does not always take the same form. A person can contribute without being the fastest texter or the main organiser.

Two fictional cases with different evidence

ObservationWhat remains unknownWhat you could do
You organise meetings; your friend attends, asks about you and suggests another day after cancelling.Whether they would like a different way to share planning.Ask them to choose the next plan before deciding all effort is one-sided.
Several invitations receive vague replies, no alternative is proposed and no meeting is agreed.Their intentions and the reason for their availability.Stop reserving time for an unconfirmed plan. Leave the next invitation with them.

These are examples, not a test of someone’s personality. Neither case creates an obligation to continue a relationship that you do not want.

Use one invitation that is easy to answer

I would enjoy seeing you again. Could you do a short coffee at [public place] on [option 1] or [option 2]? If neither works, feel free to suggest another time.

Illustrative wording. Replace brackets, remove anything untrue and send only if you want to make this invitation.

An alternative date gives you something practical to discuss. A decline, vague reply or silence means there is no agreed plan. It does not prove a particular motive. Avoid repeated follow-ups intended to force certainty.

For a friendship that already matters to you

You can describe your experience directly: “I value our friendship, but I have been finding it tiring to arrange every meeting. Would you choose our next plan?” Ask for a concrete change rather than a reassurance that everything is fine.

You can then decide what effort you want to offer. An occasional friendship or friendly acquaintance may fit better than frequent plans. You do not need to punish the person or announce a dramatic ending to stop carrying all the scheduling.

Choose your own next action

  • There is mutual effort in a different form: discuss how to share planning.
  • No plan can be agreed: keep the connection light and continue other activities.
  • A boundary is ignored or contact feels pressuring: state your boundary and seek support if you need it; meeting alone is not required.

The guide to stepping back provides wording for different levels of contact. The maintenance guide is for a connection both people want to continue.
