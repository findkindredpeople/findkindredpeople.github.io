What to say when you know nobody

Conversation starters and follow-up techniques for newcomers and shy people.

By Nadiia Honda · Updated 31 August 2026 · 7 minute read

A useful conversation opener does not need to be clever. It needs to fit the shared situation and give the other person an easy way to answer.

Begin with what is already around you

Ask how someone found the event, what they recommend to a newcomer, or whether they have tried the activity before. Shared-context questions feel natural because neither person has to reveal anything private.

Openers that work

  • “What brought you to this group?”
  • “Have you been here before?”
  • “What would you recommend to someone new to the city?”
  • “I am choosing my next weekend walk. Do you have a favorite area?”

Listen for a thread

Good conversation is less about asking many questions and more about noticing one detail. If someone mentions photography, ask what they like to photograph. If they mention a difficult move, acknowledge the feeling before changing topics.

Share, do not interview

After asking a question, add a small piece of your own experience. “I am new too; I have been trying one neighborhood each weekend.” This creates balance and gives the other person material to respond to.

End without awkwardness

You can leave warmly: “I am going to get another drink, but I am glad we spoke.” If the conversation felt mutual, offer a specific follow-up rather than requesting private contact information immediately.

Respect short answers. Interest should feel mutual, not extracted.

If you are nervous

Prepare two questions before arriving and give yourself a modest goal: speak with one person for five minutes. Confidence often arrives after action, not before it.

Common mistakes

Avoid rapid personal questions, complaints about everyone in the city, debates designed to test the other person, and pressure to exchange contact details. Curiosity works best when it includes respect for boundaries.
