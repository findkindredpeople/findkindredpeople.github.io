What to Say When You Know Nobody: English and German Examples

Words for joining a group, asking for help, handling a pause and ending a conversation without pressure.

Published by Kindred · AI-assisted content · Published 1 September 2026 · Updated 20 September 2026 · Reading guide

Choose a phrase that fits what is happening around you. The point is to make a small opening the other person can accept or leave. You do not need to get through a list of questions or keep someone talking after they want to return to the activity.

Before speaking: choose a workable moment

A break before a class, a shared task or the host’s welcome can offer a natural opening. Someone wearing headphones, following instructions, looking after another person or trying to leave may not have time. A friendly response does not by itself mean they want to exchange numbers.

About the German examples: the peer-to-peer phrases use informal du. For an organiser you do not know, the enquiry below uses formal Sie. These are suggested scripts, not quotations from real conversations.

Turn an answer into an exchange

Follow one detail they chose to share and add a related detail of your own. This gives them something to respond to without turning the conversation into an interview. Do not invent an experience to make yourself seem similar.

Illustrative exchange:
You: “Have you been to this walking group before?”
Them: “A few times. I like the shorter routes.”
You: “That would suit me too; I need to be back before dinner. Do they announce the finish time before setting off?”

The follow-up stays with the activity and a practical need. You could then listen or return to the walk. You do not need to move immediately into work, relationship status, immigration history or other private subjects.

What to do when the conversation changes

  • A short answer: pause. If they return to the task, let the exchange end. You cannot reliably tell from one answer whether someone is tired, distracted, shy or uninterested.
  • You cannot find a word: use a simpler phrase, gesture to the shared task or ask for a moment. You do not owe an apology for every language mistake.
  • They ask something too personal: “I would rather not talk about that yet. How did you find this group?” In German: “Darüber möchte ich noch nicht sprechen. Wie hast du diese Gruppe gefunden?”
  • You disagree: you can say “We see that differently” and return to the activity. A first meeting does not require resolving a debate.
  • You want to leave: use a clear, kind ending. You do not have to invent an urgent reason or stay until the other person approves.

If the group is already talking

At a suitable pause, ask “Is it okay if I join you?” or “Ist es okay, wenn ich mich dazustelle?” If the conversation is private or the response is hesitant, give them space. An organiser may be able to introduce you to an activity or another newcomer.

A formal question to the organiser

Guten Tag, ich bin zum ersten Mal dabei. Können Sie mir sagen, wie ich am besten einsteigen kann? Mein Deutsch ist noch nicht so sicher. Gibt es eine Aktivität, bei der ich zunächst mitmachen und zuhören kann?

Meaning: “Hello, this is my first time. Could you tell me how best to join in? I am not very confident in German yet. Is there an activity where I can take part and listen at first?”

Ask about another meeting only when you want one

If you enjoyed the exchange, suggest a small specific plan: “Would you like to get a coffee after next Thursday’s session?” Leave room for no. If they agree, confirm the date and place; if they decline, accept the answer without bargaining.

A useful result for today

Pick one situation from the table and keep a phrase you can say in your own words. Afterwards, ask whether the exchange felt comfortable and whether you want to return. The length of the conversation or the number of contacts you collected is not a score.
